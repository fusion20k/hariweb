Why I Built Hari

A personal tool made for real reconnection

My girlfriend is Filipino-American, and she's always wanted to actually learn Tagalog, not just understand bits of it from family gatherings — to actually hold a conversation with her lola on the phone instead of waiting for someone to translate. But between work and school, she never had a real block of time to sit down with a course or grind through an app every night.

It wasn't that she didn't want it badly enough. It was that every option out there expects you to carve out extra time for it. So I tried building the opposite: something that works with the time she already spends online instead of asking for more of it.

That turned into Hari. It quietly swaps in Tagalog words while she's browsing like she normally would, so she's picking things up on the same sites she was already scrolling through anyway, one tab and one sentence at a time — slowly closing the gap between understanding more Tagalog than she could actually speak.

Hari works across the modern web — news, Wikipedia, blogs, Reddit, social feeds, and most English-language pages. A small number of sites with unusual layouts or strict extension policies may not be supported, but Hari covers the vast majority of everyday browsing.

Switching your system language just relabels a handful of menus and settings. It doesn't touch anything you actually read on websites. And dropping straight into a fully Tagalog interface with zero support is overwhelming — most people switch it back within a day. Hari does the opposite: your interface stays exactly the same, and Tagalog shows up inside the words you're already reading, at whatever pace works for you.

Duolingo works for a lot of people, but it asks you to set aside time every day for isolated words and grammar drills, and that's exactly the kind of routine that's easy to fall off of. Hari doesn't ask you to change your schedule at all. It just puts Tagalog into the browsing you're already doing, so there's no lesson to sit through and no streak to keep alive.
