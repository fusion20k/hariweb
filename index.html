Why I Built Hari

A personal tool made for real reconnection

My girlfriend is Filipino-American. Like many Fil-Ams, she felt a deep desire to learn Tagalog and reconnect with her heritage. But with a busy work and study schedule, finding hours for traditional courses, grammar drilling, or maintaining app streaks felt impossible.

I realized the problem wasn't her dedication—it was the tools. Traditional methods ask you to change your life to fit language learning. I wanted to build something that did the opposite: a tool that fits language learning into your existing life.

I built Hari to let her acquire Tagalog vocabulary passively while browsing the web. By quietly swapping English words for Tagalog on the sites she already uses, she was able to start reconnecting with her roots, one click, one tab, and one sentence at a time.

Switching your system language only changes a handful of menu labels and settings — it doesn't touch the actual content you read on websites. Worse, it drops you into a fully Tagalog interface with no scaffolding, which most learners find overwhelming and quickly revert. Hari takes the opposite approach: it leaves your familiar interface intact and weaves Tagalog into the words you naturally read each day. That's where real comprehension builds — inside context you already understand, at a pace that scales with you.

Active app-drilling streaks ask you to carve out dedicated time to review isolated words and artificial grammar drills, which is exhausting to maintain and often fails to build real-world fluency. Hari is different: it fits language acquisition into your existing daily habits by weaving Tagalog directly into your natural web browsing. There are no lessons, no stakes, and no streaks to maintain—just passive, context-first immersion that scales naturally with your comprehension.
