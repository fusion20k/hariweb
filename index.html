Why I built Hari

A personal tool made for real reconnection

My girlfriend is Filipino-American, and she's always wanted to actually learn Tagalog, not just understand bits of it from family gatherings. She wanted to hold a conversation with her lola on the phone instead of waiting for someone to translate. But between work and school, she never had a real block of time to sit down with a course or grind through an app every night.

It wasn't that she didn't want it badly enough. It was that every option out there expects you to carve out extra time for it. So I tried building the opposite: something that works with the time she already spends online instead of asking for more of it.

That turned into Hari. It quietly swaps in Tagalog words while she browses like she normally would, so she picks things up on the same sites she was already scrolling through, one tab and one sentence at a time.

Salamat,Daniel “Jack”

Every account includes 15,000 Tagalog characters monthly — roughly 5 to 10 full articles or blog posts' worth of translated reading, depending on length. For most people browsing a few times a week, that covers a normal month before you'd ever see a limit. Your allocation refreshes automatically at the start of each cycle.

Hari pauses translations until your next monthly cycle begins. We'll let you know, and you can upgrade to Premium (1,000,000 characters) or switch to Pay As You Go if you want more. We never charge you without your say-so.

Hari is built to be lightweight and stay out of a page's way — it runs locally in your browser, only touches the text it translates, and doesn't load extra scripts or ads that would slow a page down. If something ever looks off, you can turn Hari off for that site in one click.

Switching your system language just relabels a handful of menus and settings. It doesn't touch anything you actually read on websites.
