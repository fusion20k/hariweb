Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need to already know some Tagalog?

No. Hari is built for people starting from zero. You set the slider low, and it introduces just a few Tagalog words at a time so your brain can absorb them in context. You'll start recognizing words naturally — no memorization required.

I've tried apps before and burned out. Is this different?

Yes — because Hari doesn't ask you to open anything new or set aside study time. It lives in your browser and works quietly while you do what you already do online. There's nothing to maintain, no streaks to keep, no guilt if you miss a day.

Which sites does Hari work on?

Hari works on most text-based websites — news sites, Twitter/X, Facebook, blogs, and more. Some platforms with heavy restrictions (like certain streaming services) may be limited.

Why isn't Hari completely free?

Each page you browse with Hari on uses paid translation APIs. The $20/month subscription covers those costs so we can keep Hari focused on quality.

Can I cancel anytime?

Yes. Cancel from your account dashboard in a couple of clicks — no long-term commitment, no questions asked. Cancel before your trial ends and you won't be charged at all.

Why do I need a card for the trial?

Hari uses paid translation APIs, so every page you translate has a real cost. Requiring a card prevents abuse and means your subscription can continue automatically after your trial without any interruption — if Hari is working for you. You can cancel before your trial ends and you won't be charged.

What happens when I hit 10,000 characters?

Hari will let you know inside the extension and by email as you approach the limit. Once you hit 10,000 characters, translations pause. If you stay subscribed, translations pick right back up at $20/month. If you cancel, they stop and you won't be charged again.

Does this work if I only know English?

Yes — Hari is made specifically for Fil-Ams who grew up in English. You set how much Tagalog appears (starting at just 10% means only a handful of words per page), so you never get overwhelmed. The goal is steady, low-pressure exposure, not a crash course.
